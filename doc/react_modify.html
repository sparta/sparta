react_modify command

Syntax: 

react_modify keyword values ...
  • one or more keyword/value pairs may be listed
  • keywords = recomb or rboost or compute_chem_rates or partial_energy or elec_energy or vib_energy 
      recomb value = yes or no = enable or disable defined recombination reactions
  rboost value = rfactor
    rfactor = boost probability of recombination reactions by this factor
  compute_chem_rates value = yes or no = enable or disable computation of Arrhenius rate for chemical
  reaction without performing the reaction
  partial_energy = yes or no = use partial energy or total energy for TCE chemistry
  elec_energy = yes or no or micro = how electronic energy couples to the TCE reaction probability
  vib_energy = dof or micro = how discrete vibrational energy couples to the TCE reaction probability

Examples: 

react_modify recomb no
react_modify rboost 100.0

Description:

Set parameters that affect how reactions are performed.

The recomb keyword turns on or off recombination reactions. It is only relevant if recombination reactions were defined in the reaction file read in by the react command. If the setting is no then they will be disabled even if they were listed in the reaction file. This is useful to turn recombination reactions off, to see if they affect simulation results.

The rboost keyword is a setting for recombination reactions. It is ignored if no recombination reactions exist, or the recomb keyword is set to no. The rboost setting does not affect the overalll statistical results of recombination reactions, but tries to improve their computational efficiency. Recombination reactions typically occur with very low probability, which means the code spends time testing for reactions that rarely occur. If the rfactor is set to N > 1, then recombination reactions are skipped N-1 out of N times, when one or more such reactions is defined for a pair of colliding particles. A random number us used to select on that probability. To compensate, when a recombination reaction is actually tested for occurrence, its rate is boosted by a factor of N, making it N times more likely to occur.

The smallest value rboost can be set to is 1.0, which effectively applies no boost factor.

IMPORTANT NOTE: Setting rboost too large could meant the probability of a recombination reaction becomes > 1.0, when it is does occur. SPARTA does not check for this, so you should estimate the largest boost factor that is safe to use for your model.

The compute_chem_rates keyword is a setting that allows the user to only compute Arrhenius rates for chemical reactions without performing them. Currently only the TCE reaction model supports this keyword; an error will occur when using the QK or TCE/QK reaction model with this keyword.

The partial_energy keyword is a setting that allows the user to choose the amount of internal energy and internal degrees of freedom used in the TCE model.

If the partial_energy keyword is set to yes, the rDOF model of Bird is used, and only the sum of the relative translational energy between the partcles and a fraction of the rotational energy is used. The participating internal degrees of freedom are either set to 1 (dissociation reactions), or 0 (recombination, exchange, ionization reactions).

Conversely, if the partial_energy keyword is set to no, then the total energy model is used, i.e. the sum of the relative translational energy between the partcles and the rotational and vibrational energies. The participating internal degrees of freedom are computed directly by the code and do not need to be inputted by the user. The vibrational energy model used has an impact on the internal degrees of freedom used in the TCE model in that case. This option is ignored for the QK reaction model.

The elec_energy keyword controls whether electronic energy (see the elecfile option of the species command and fix elecmode) counts toward the TCE reaction energy when partial_energy is set to no.

If elec_energy is set to no, the electronic energy of the colliding pair is excluded from the collision energy compared against the reaction threshold, and the per-state electronic degrees of freedom listed in the electronic species file are ignored. Electronic energy is still conserved: it is part of the total collision energy that is redistributed over all modes of the products after a reaction. This is the consistent choice for standard equilibrium-calibrated rate sets (Park, Gupta, etc.), whose Arrhenius coefficients already contain the equilibrium excited-state contribution; counting electronic energy again makes the equilibrium TCE rate exceed the input Arrhenius rate, most strongly at low temperature.

If elec_energy is set to yes, the historical behavior is restored: electronic energy is added to the reaction energy and the per-state electronic degrees of freedom from the electronic species file contribute to the internal degree-of-freedom count. This asserts that every quantum of electronic energy lowers the reaction barrier one-for-one, which is only appropriate if the input rates are true ground-state-specific rates.

If elec_energy is set to micro (the default), electronic energy counts toward the reaction barrier AND the equilibrium rates still reproduce the input Arrhenius data: the energy-dependent factor of the TCE probability is replaced by its microcanonical average over the two reactants' electronic ladders at fixed total collision energy, 

sum_p g_p (Ec - eps_p - Ea)^(z+eta+1/2) / sum_p g_p (Ec - eps_p)^(z+3/2-omega)

where Ec is the total collision energy including electronic, and p runs over the pairs of electronic states with energies eps_p and degeneracies g_p. This average keeps the equilibrium rate on the input Arrhenius data across temperature. Out of equilibrium the reaction rate then responds to the actual electronic populations in both directions: an electronically cold gas reacts more slowly than the thermal rate (the tabulated rate contains the equilibrium excited-state contribution) and an electronically hot gas reacts faster, with excited particles preferentially selected. The per-state DOF column of the electronic species file is ignored in this mode. The exactness holds for a fixed internal degree-of-freedom count; with discrete vibration the instantaneous vibrational DOF fluctuates per collision, which shifts equilibrium rates by a few percent (comparable to the discrete-vibration TCE deviation that exists without electronic states). Setting vib_energy to micro removes that residual as well. When the colliding species carry no electronic states, this factor reduces to the standard TCE factor, so micro leaves non-electronic runs unchanged and is the default.

The vib_energy keyword controls how discrete vibrational energy enters the TCE reaction probability when partial_energy is set to no and collide_modify vibrate discrete is used.

If vib_energy is set to dof (the default), the historical behavior is used: vibrational energy is added to the collision energy and each molecule contributes an instantaneous vibrational degree-of-freedom count evaluated from its current vibrational energy. Because that DOF count fluctuates from collision to collision, the equilibrium TCE rate deviates from the input Arrhenius rate; for typical air dissociation reactions the deviation reaches tens of percent at low temperature (e.g. +27% at 10000 K for O2/N2 dissociation).

If vib_energy is set to micro, the energy-dependent factor of the TCE probability is replaced by its microcanonical average over the reactants' discrete vibrational ladders (simple harmonic levels of each vibrational mode) at fixed total collision energy, in the same way that elec_energy micro averages over the electronic ladder. The average is precomputed once per reaction as a lookup table, so the runtime cost is one table interpolation per potential reaction. The equilibrium TCE rate then reproduces the input Arrhenius rate essentially exactly at all temperatures, and out of equilibrium the rate responds to the actual vibrational populations with no fitted parameters (a vibrationally cold gas reacts more slowly than the thermal rate, a vibrationally hot gas faster). If elec_energy is also set to micro and electronic species data is defined, the table averages over the joint vibrational-electronic ladder, making the two modes consistent with each other.

The vib_energy micro setting requires the TCE reaction model with partial_energy no and collide_modify vibrate discrete, and cannot be combined with elec_energy yes.

Restrictions: none

Related commands:

react

Default:

The option defaults are recomb = yes, rboost = 1000.0, compute_chem_rates = no, partial_energy = yes, elec_energy = micro, vib_energy = dof. The elec_energy micro default reduces to the standard TCE factor when no electronic states are present, so it only affects runs with electronic excitation. For those runs, vib_energy micro additionally removes the discrete-vibration TCE rate deviation.
